    Ledger

    Schema registry and migration runner · Northlight · Go, Postgres

    Northlight had forty services sharing eleven databases and no agreed way to change a column. Migrations went out on a weekly release train, which meant a one-line change waited five days and a bad one took the whole train down.

    Ledger makes every schema change a reviewed artifact: it records the intended end state, plans a reversible path to get there, and refuses plans that would lock a hot table. It runs migrations behind a feature-flagged dual-write window so a rollback is a flag flip rather than an incident.

    • Median time from merged migration to production: 5 days → 40 minutes.
    • Zero migration-caused incidents in the eight months since rollout.
    • Adopted by 6 of 8 product teams without a mandate, which I am proud of.

    Tideline

    Terminal trace explorer · Open source · Rust

    During a long outage I got tired of paging through a web trace UI on a laptop tethered to a phone. Tideline reads OpenTelemetry spans and renders them as a navigable flame graph in the terminal, with filtering by service, duration, and error status.

    I expected to be the only user. It is now at 2.1k stars with contributors in four countries, and maintaining it has taught me more about receiving code review than writing it.

    • Rust
    • OpenTelemetry
    • CLI

  • 2023

    Quarry

    Reading tool · Side project · TypeScript, SQLite

    Save an article, get it back when it matters. Quarry strips articles to text, indexes them for full-text search, and mails a weekly digest of what you saved but never read. Everything is stored in a single SQLite file you can download and take with you.

    About 400 people use it. It costs eleven dollars a month to run, which feels like the right shape for a side project.

    Halyard

    Payments reconciliation · Meridian Labs · Python, Kafka

    A reconciliation engine matching card settlements against the internal ledger across six processors. The hard part was never the matching — it was representing the twenty ways a transaction can be legitimately unmatched and making that legible to a finance team at 6am.

    • Cut the manual reconciliation queue from ~900 items a day to under 40.
    • Handled roughly $2B in annualized volume by the time I left.

    • Kafka

    Foundry

    Co-founder · Shut down 2021

    A collaborative environment for data teams. We built something people liked demoing and did not need. We had 14 months of runway, honest early users, and no path from “this is neat” to “I would be upset if this went away.”

    We returned what was left of the money and wound it down. I learned more in that year than in the three before it, most of it about talking to customers before writing code.
